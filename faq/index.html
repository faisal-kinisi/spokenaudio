Recording

Start, pause, carry on

REC starts a recording. Tap REC again to pause; RESUME (middle of the bottom row) carries on where you stopped. LENGTH at the top shows the recording’s length.

Your words as you speak

SpokenAudio writes your words while you dictate, on your iPhone (Apple’s speech engine). Words in italics are still being worked out; they settle a moment later.

Microphone

The mic button at the top left shows the microphone in use. Tap it to choose another (Apple’s microphone list). With “Same as System” the mic follows the headset you’re wearing.

Auto-pause

If no voice is heard for the time chosen in Settings ▸ Recording, you get a 30-second warning, then the recording pauses. Nothing is lost.

Long recordings

Recordings of several hours are fine. The audio is saved, encrypted, as you record, so a crash or a call doesn’t lose it (see If a recording stops unexpectedly).

Recording effect

Settings ▸ Voice screen ▸ Recording effect. Only for the look — the recording is never affected.

  • Off — the wheel as it is.
  • Voice — a picture on the wheel that moves with your voice while you record: flat in silence, taller when you speak louder.
  • Glow only — no picture; the wheel glows while something is happening.
  • Voice + glow — both.
  • Voice style — Strands, Ribbons, Silk (thin lines twisting into one ribbon), Aurora (soft glowing bands), 3D wave, Neon horizon (a colour wave over a grid floor), Bars (level bars behind REC), Dot matrix (columns of dots), Skyline (glowing level shapes on a grid floor), Code rain (bits of light rising out of a mist), Mist threads (threads of light rising and falling) or Burst (red rays from under REC). With a picture on, REC turns see-through so it shows. Neon horizon is the default.
  • Full page — with the transcript on the full page the wheel is hidden: the picture plays inside REC and the glow rings REC.
  • Strength — Subtle, Medium or Full — how tall and bright the picture and the glow are; at Full the glow also reaches further.
  • Reduce Motion — with iPhone Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Motion ▸ Reduce Motion on, nothing moves and nothing breathes.
What the colours mean

Only while something is happening; never when paused.

  • Recording — REC glows red; with a glow on, the wheel breathes cyan and its ring turns slowly.
  • Playing — anything playing, also while paused in a recording: a steady cyan glow, and PLAY glows green.
  • Turning the wheel — paused in a recording: steady amber with OVR, steady green with INS.
  • Recording an edit — amber breathing for OVR, green for INS; the pill’s dot glows too.

Editing with OVR and INS

OVR — record over

Put the cursor where the change starts, choose OVR, tap REC and speak. The new words replace the old ones for as long as you speak. On the full page the words being replaced are struck through as you go; at the pause you see exactly what was replaced.

INS — add in

Choose INS and tap REC: your new words go in at the cursor and everything after it moves along. Nothing is replaced.

Switching OVR and INS

Choose before you tap REC. While the microphone records, the pill can’t be switched (pause first). During an edit, the dot of OVR or INS glows.

A paused take

While a take is paused, REC at the same place carries it on. It becomes an edit when you record somewhere else, switch OVR/INS, press Undo or Stop.

Undo and Redo

Undo takes back the last edit, up to 100. Redo puts it back — until you record something new, which ends Redo.

[word]

A word in brackets is one an edit’s cut fell inside. Listen to it and check it.

Where edits are

Amber marks OVR, green marks INS, in the text and as ticks on the progress bar. Hold a finger on a tick to go to that edit.

Edit a saved recording

In Sessions, swipe left on it and tap Edit (or Edit recording in its details). You work on a copy; the original changes only when you save.

Moving around a recording

Three views

Pull the bar under the strip down or push it up: Strip (one moving line), Lines (a few lines above the strip) and Page (the whole text). Tap the bar to step through them.

The wheel

Turn it to wind through the recording; you hear the voice as you go (Settings ▸ Playback ▸ Scrub sound: Oxide, Electro or Silent).

Skip buttons

Either side of the wheel, and at the edges of the Page view.

  • Tap — one word.
  • Double tap — previous or next sentence or edit (or 10 / 30 seconds, Settings ▸ Playback).
  • Hold — the beginning or the end.
On the page

Paused: tap a word to put the cursor after it. Playing: tap a word to play from it; hold to stop there. While recording, taps do nothing (pause first).

Progress bar

Drag to move; the length is at its right end.

Playback speed

Settings ▸ Playback.

Stop, save and drafts

STOP asks what to do
  • Save — keeps the recording (a copy you edited replaces the original).
  • Save As — keeps it as a new recording; the original stays as it was, and the copy keeps the original’s recording date.
  • Keep as draft — keeps it unfinished in Sessions ▸ Drafts, to continue later.
  • Discard / Discard changes — throws away this session’s work; an edited recording goes back to its last save.
Drafts

Continue a draft with Continue (swipe left on it), rename it, or Save it. Drafts can be deleted automatically after 7, 30 or 90 days (Settings ▸ Recovery & storage); saved recordings never are.

The “i” button

Left of +, for a recording that is open: when it was recorded and last saved, its length, words, size, edits, how many you can undo, words flagged for review. While you edit, it shows what has changed since the last save.

Sessions

Your recordings

All, Saved and Drafts, each with its count (All = saved + drafts). Tap ▶ to play one in the Voice tab, the page icon to read it in Words.

List style

Detailed or Simple (the list icon at the top).

Sort

By last change, date recorded, name, length, words or size, either way round (the ↕ icon). Sorted by size, each row shows its size.

Swipes
  • Swipe right — Export.
  • Swipe left on a saved recording — Edit, Rename, Delete.
  • Swipe left on a draft — Continue, Rename, Save, Delete. Renaming a draft keeps it a draft.
Details

Tap a row: rename, play, edit, open the transcript, re-transcribe from the audio, the modification record, delete the audio or the transcript only, or delete the recording.

Re-transcribe

Writes the words again from the audio. The earlier words are kept.

Size

An edited recording keeps its original audio and every take, so it uses about twice the space of the audio alone.

Words

Read

The Words tab shows the transcript of the recording open in Voice (or the one you picked in Sessions), in paragraphs.

Share

The share button sends the text (after the Security Notice). Text selection and copying are off on purpose: words leave SpokenAudio only through Share.

No transcript?

Re-transcribe it from its audio right there.

Modification record

What it keeps

Every OVR and INS made in a recording: its number, when it was made, who made it, where, how long, the words removed and added, and whether it is in the recording, undone, or was dropped (undone, then ended by new recording).

Where

The “i” button ▸ Modification record, or Sessions ▸ details ▸ Modification record. Detailed list style shows every edit with its words; Simple shows one line per edit, with “Show all changes” for undone and dropped edits.

Author

Your name from Settings ▸ Profile ▸ Name (“SA user” if blank). Changing it later does not change edits already made.

Older recordings

Edits made before the record existed are marked “before the record”: date, place and length are known; words only for the last 100 edits.

Encrypted

The record holds your words, so it is encrypted like your audio. In an export, the Word document’s record table shows the words each edit removed and added; the spreadsheet file (.csv) gives dates, times, places, lengths and counts — no words.

Exporting and sharing

Export

Swipe right in Sessions, or Export in details. Several recordings at once: select, then Export. Choose any of:

  • Word document (.docx) — the transcript; with Full, also the modification record table and the audio checksum.
  • PDF (.pdf) — the same content as the Word document, to read, print or sign; with Full or Custom it ends with “Reviewed and approved”, signature and date lines and your Default signature (Settings ▸ Profile; the lines stay when it is blank).
  • Plain text (.txt) — the transcript as plain text. Plain text can’t hold a password, so with the password on it goes in the encrypted .7z — also when there is no audio.
  • Audio (.7z) — the recording in a .7z archive — with the modification record as a spreadsheet file (.csv) when the recording was edited.
What the documents show

Above the password step:

  • Full — the default: markups, the modification record, the audio checksum (SHA-256) and the record as a spreadsheet (.csv).
  • Clean copy — the final words only — no markups, no record, no checksum, no .csv. The record stays in SpokenAudio.
  • Custom — switch each of the four on or off; SpokenAudio remembers your choice.
Password protect

On for every export: the Word document and the PDF are encrypted with AES-256, and the audio, the plain text and the .csv go in one .7z archive encrypted with AES-256, with the names of the files inside hidden too. One password opens every file of that export.

Without a password

You can turn protection off for one export; SpokenAudio asks every time and never remembers it. Every file is then plain, the text and the .csv too. Anyone who gets those files can read and hear everything — don’t use it for patient, client or other confidential information.

The password

A new one for each export, made by your iPhone:

  • Random (16) — 16 letters and digits in four groups, like K7mQ-4tXw-9hRc-2pLz. The dashes are part of it.
  • Random words (7) — seven words joined by dashes — easier to read out over the phone.
  • My own — at least 15 characters; avoid words, names, dates and keyboard runs. Use plain dashes and quotes: a recipient typing on a computer can’t type the long dash or curly quotes iPhone may put in.
A new password isn’t kept

A new password is shown once, after the export, and SpokenAudio doesn’t keep it (only a password you save under Saved export password is kept). If it is lost, the files can’t be opened — by anyone, including SpokenAudio.

After export

Copy puts the password on this iPhone’s clipboard only (not your other devices) for 60 seconds. Print prints the password and the export ID only. Send the password a different way from the files — a call, a text, in person — and keep it in a password manager app or another secure place.

Saved export password

Settings ▸ Advanced ▸ Saved export password keeps one password for every export, on this iPhone only, with Face ID to see, use, replace or remove it. Convenient, but if it ever leaks it opens every file you have sent with it; a new password for each export limits a leak to one export.

Opening the files

The recipient needs the password and:

  • Word document — Microsoft Word (current versions) asks for the password. Some other apps can’t open password-protected Word files.
  • PDF — Adobe Acrobat Reader, Preview on Mac, Chrome and the iPhone Files app ask for the password. A few older PDF apps can’t open AES-256 PDFs.
  • Audio archive — an app that opens 7z archives encrypted with AES-256 — for example 7-Zip on Windows, or Keka or The Unarchiver on Mac. Some built-in tools, such as the iPhone Files app, can’t open it.
  • On iPhone or iPad — an unzip app that opens password-protected 7z archives.
  • Password managers — any password manager app works: it only stores the password, so its encryption doesn’t need to match the files’.
Markups

Word: your edits arrive as Word’s own Track Changes — accept or reject each one in Word. Each OVR and INS is a revision with its author and time: removed words struck through, added words underlined. PDF: removed words struck through in red, added words underlined in green, with the edit’s number from the record. Plain text: [brackets] round words to check. Markups off: the final words only. An edit that can’t be placed exactly in the text is listed in the record and the document says so.

Audio checksum (SHA-256)

A checksum is a 64-character fingerprint of a file’s exact bytes. Change one byte and it is completely different — so it shows whether an audio file is the one this transcript was made from, and that it wasn’t changed. The Word document, the PDF, the plain text and the checksum file give:

  • The recording as WAV — always. Any WAV export of this version of the recording gives the same fingerprint. An edit (OVR or INS) changes the audio, so it changes the fingerprint — that is the point.
  • The audio file in this export — when the audio goes with it, in the format you chose. This one matches only that file.
  • Why WAV, ALAC and M4A differ — they store the same recording differently: WAV as is, ALAC packed smaller with nothing lost, M4A smallest with tiny changes you can’t hear. Different bytes, different fingerprints. ALAC and M4A files also record when they were written, so exporting one again gives a new fingerprint; a WAV export gives the same one every time.
  • The checksum file — with the audio, a short checksum .txt goes too (inside the .7z with a password) giving the same values, so they’re at hand next to the audio. It is a convenience copy that could be changed along with the audio; the password-protected Word document or PDF is the record to rely on.
  • To check a file — Windows, with 7-Zip: right-click the file → CRC SHA → SHA-256 (on Windows 11, Show more options first), or PowerShell: Get-FileHash and the file. Mac: a checksum app, or Terminal: shasum -a 256 and the file. Compare with the document; capital letters don’t matter. A different result means a different or changed file.
  • Where it is safe — inside the password-protected Word document and PDF. The .7z archive can’t show a change by itself.
File names are not protected

Anyone who sees the files sees their names. Each export offers a name with no personal details, like SA-7KQ4M2 2026-11-08 14.32; if you type one that may identify someone (a name, a birth date, a record number), you are asked first.

Audio format

Under Audio, choose:

  • WAV — an exact copy; the largest file; opens everywhere.
  • ALAC — an exact copy (Apple Lossless, .m4a), about half the size of WAV.
  • M4A — the smallest (AAC) — a small fraction of the WAV; very slightly changed, with no difference you’d hear in speech.
Exact copies for records

For legal or medical records, an exact copy (WAV or ALAC) is the safer choice. Your recordings inside SpokenAudio always stay exact, whatever you export.

Where to

Share files… opens the share panel: Mail, Messages, AirDrop, or Save to Files — which includes iCloud Drive and any cloud drive app you have set up in Files (Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox). The files are deleted from this iPhone when sharing finishes.

Word list

Security and privacy

On your iPhone

Speech is turned into words on your iPhone. No audio or text is sent anywhere unless you export or share it. iCloud or computer backups of this iPhone include SpokenAudio’s files, still encrypted.

Encrypted

Audio, words, and the modification record are encrypted with AES-256-GCM as they are saved. The key is kept in this iPhone’s Keychain and can’t be used on any other iPhone.

A new iPhone, or a backup

Recordings can’t be opened on another iPhone — not even from an iCloud or computer backup restored to a new iPhone. Before you change iPhones, export the recordings you want to keep (Sessions ▸ Export). Destroy encryption keys makes them unreadable on this iPhone.

Not in the Files app

SpokenAudio’s files can’t be browsed in the Files app; they leave only through Export, or Share on the Words tab.

Lock

Settings ▸ Security ▸ Lock SpokenAudio: Face ID (or your passcode) to open SpokenAudio. It locks:

  • When it opens — every time.
  • When you leave it — at once — unless it is recording.
  • After the auto-lock time — without a touch, also while a recording is paused (“Never”: only when it opens and when you leave it).
  • Never while recording — dictation is never interrupted by the lock.
Above everything

The lock covers every open page, sheet and share panel; after Face ID you are back exactly where you were. With VoiceOver, only the lock screen is read.

Delete and Export ask again

With the lock on, deleting a recording, a draft, its audio or its transcript asks for Face ID or your passcode every time; exporting, and sharing a transcript from the Words tab, ask once every 5 minutes, and again after SpokenAudio locks. Lock off: nothing is asked.

App switcher

When you switch apps, SpokenAudio shows only its icon — never your words, recording names or lengths. The same while a call, Siri or Control Center is on top.

Keyboard

Keyboards from other apps aren’t supported yet — SpokenAudio uses Apple’s keyboard. A keyboard from another app, once given Full Access, can send what you type to its maker; inside SpokenAudio, what you type (recording names, passwords, your name) never goes to one.

Terms, privacy and consent

Recording other people

Many places, including California, require everyone being recorded to agree first. Tell people and get their consent before you record them — that is yours to do, not SpokenAudio’s.

Check every transcript

Speech recognition makes mistakes: words, names, numbers and doses can be misheard or missed. Check the transcript against the audio before you rely on it. SpokenAudio is a tool, not medical or legal advice.

Your rules

Patient, client and confidentiality rules (HIPAA, privilege, GDPR) are yours to follow when you record, export and share.

Terms, Privacy, Security

Settings ▸ Legal, or Support on the Help page: the Terms of Use, the Privacy Policy, and Security and standards (the published encryption standards SpokenAudio uses). They open with no internet, and are also at spokenaudio.com.

What you agreed

Settings ▸ Legal shows the Terms version you agreed to and when, and when you agreed to the first-export notice. It is kept on this iPhone only; we never receive it.

If a recording stops unexpectedly

Restore

If SpokenAudio stops during a recording (crash, battery, a call), the next time it opens it asks: Restore, or Keep as draft. The audio recorded up to that moment is kept either way.

  • Restore — opens it in the Voice tab, writes its words again from the audio, and you carry on.
  • Keep as draft — leaves it in Sessions ▸ Drafts; Continue there does the same as Restore later.
Export draft audio

Settings ▸ Advanced — only for a draft that won’t open. It copies the draft’s audio out as plain audio files to share, with its modification record and a read-me that says how the pieces fit. It never changes the draft and brings nothing back into SpokenAudio. A second that can’t be read is written as silence, so everything still lines up.

Storage used

Settings ▸ Recovery & storage: saved recordings and drafts, every file.

Free trial and unlock

7 days free

Every feature is free for 7 days from when you tap Start free trial. The trial belongs to your Apple account: another iPhone, or deleting and reinstalling SpokenAudio, continues the same 7 days. Sessions and Settings ▸ SpokenAudio show the days left.

After the trial

You can still open, play, read and export every recording and draft, with or without a password.

  • Needs the unlock — recording, OVR and INS editing, Edit and Continue, re-transcribing, and changing settings.
  • Settings — stay exactly as you set them and keep working that way. The app lock, Export draft audio and Destroy all encryption keys can still be used.
  • A recording in progress — is never cut off: if the trial ends while you record, carry on until you stop, and it is saved.
Unlock

One purchase, no subscription. It unlocks SpokenAudio on every iPhone signed in to the same Apple account. Apple shows the price for your country when you unlock.

Restore purchase

On the Welcome screen, the unlock screen and Settings ▸ SpokenAudio. Use it on a new iPhone, or after reinstalling, if SpokenAudio doesn’t show Unlocked by itself.

Your recordings stay on this iPhone

The unlock goes with your Apple account; recordings don’t move to your other devices or a new iPhone. Export the ones you want to keep elsewhere, and keep the passwords of protected exports safe: without them those files can’t be opened, by anyone.

Saved export password

Before the trial starts and after it ends, each export uses a new password. My saved password comes back when you unlock.

Ask to Buy

If a parent approves purchases, the screen says it is waiting. SpokenAudio unlocks by itself when the purchase is approved.

Earlier version

If you used the earlier version of SpokenAudio on this iPhone, a fresh 7-day trial starts automatically when you first open this version (until 31 January 2027).

Advanced settings

Transcript sync offset

Moves the highlighted word earlier or later against the voice (default −0.25 s).

Faster live words

Shows words sooner while you dictate; Apple notes they may be slightly less accurate.

Verify encryption

Checks that encryption works on this iPhone.

Export draft audio

See If a recording stops unexpectedly. Choose the drafts and a format (WAV, ALAC or M4A); you see the size and the free space first; you can leave the screen while it runs, and Cancel stops it. Then Share or Delete.

Exported files are not encrypted

Files you don’t share are deleted when you tap Delete, and otherwise the next time SpokenAudio opens.

Saved export password

Set, change or remove the one password “My saved password” uses when you export. See Exporting and sharing.

Share app log

Settings ▸ Advanced ▸ Share app log: SpokenAudio’s own log, for support if something goes wrong — times, numbers and settings, never your words, recording names or device names. Choose the last 24 hours or everything kept (about two weeks), then send it the way you choose. Nothing is sent unless you share it.

Rescued files on this iPhone

Shown only if an earlier version left plain (not encrypted) audio copies of drafts on this iPhone. They are kept out of iPhone backups; Delete removes them (Face ID or passcode with the lock on). Your drafts are not affected, but a copy of a draft you have since deleted is the last copy of that audio.

Destroy encryption keys

Makes every recording and transcript unreadable on this iPhone, and removes the saved export password. Only when you retire this iPhone. SpokenAudio has no undo; a backup of this iPhone made earlier may still contain them.

Return to default settings

Puts settings back as they came. Your recordings, your profile and your lock are not touched.
